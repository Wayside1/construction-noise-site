Environmental Health Officers are not short of complaints about construction noise. What they are short of is evidence they can act on. The difference between a complaint that gets a site visit and one that gets a polite reply is almost always the quality of the record.

## What an officer actually needs

Under the Environmental Protection Act 1990, a council must decide whether noise is a **statutory nuisance** — an unreasonable and substantial interference with the use of your home. To judge that, an officer needs to know:

- When the noise happens, and whether it is predictable
- How long each episode lasts
- How loud it is relative to the area's normal background
- Whether it interferes with sleep, work or normal use of your home

## The five-part log

Keep a simple written record for each episode. A notebook works, but a spreadsheet with one row per episode keeps it consistent.

1. **Date and start time.** Note the exact minute the noise began.
2. **End time.** If it ran all afternoon, say so. Duration matters more than intensity.
3. **Noise type.** Drilling, hammering, machinery, demolition, reversing alarms, or a combination.
4. **Location.** Where you were and where the sound came from.
5. **Impact.** "Could not take a work call between 09:15 and 10:00" is more useful than "very loud".

## Taking measurements that help

Smartphone sound level apps are not calibrated instruments, and no officer will treat them as proof. They are still valuable as corroboration when used consistently.

- Take readings from the same position every time
- Record at least 60 seconds per measurement and note the average, not the peak
- Capture the time on screen with a screenshot
- Note the weather: wind on a microphone inflates readings significantly

## Video is your strongest single item

A 30-second clip that shows the site, the activity and your own property boundary is worth a great deal. It demonstrates that the noise is actually coming from the site you are complaining about.

**Do not edit the video.** Export it from the phone and keep the original.

## How to send it

Send a short covering email with the log attached as a spreadsheet or PDF. State what you want: an assessment of whether the noise amounts to a statutory nuisance, and a site visit during the activity in question.

If the council does not act and you believe the noise is a statutory nuisance, you retain the right to bring proceedings yourself in a magistrates' court under Section 82 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990, after giving the person responsible at least three days' written notice (the notice period for noise under section 82(7)).
