## Why is marine construction noise different?

Ports, quays, jetties, bridges over estuaries and sea defences are often built right next to some of the most protected wildlife sites in the country. That changes who you answer to. As well as the council, the work may be controlled by a **marine licence** from the Marine Management Organisation (MMO), issued under the Marine and Coastal Access Act 2009, and by **Natural England** where it affects a Site of Special Scientific Interest (SSSI) or a protected bird site.

It also means dealing with two kinds of noise at once.

| | Underwater noise | Airborne noise |
|---|---|---|
| **Main sources** | Impact piling into the bed, dredging, vessels | Piling hammers, breakers, generators, pumps and plant on quays and decks |
| **Who it affects** | Fish (such as migrating salmon), harbour porpoise, seals in the water | Nesting or feeding birds on mudflats and saltmarsh, seals hauled out, nearby homes |
| **Usual controls** | Bubble curtains and other noise abatement systems, soft starts, marine mammal observers | Tall acoustic walls, shrouds on the noisiest plant, timing and sequencing |
| **Does an acoustic wall help?** | No | Yes, if it is high enough and close enough to the noise |

Getting this split right is the most important step. A wall on the quay will not satisfy a licence condition about underwater noise, and a bubble curtain will not protect birds on the mudflats from a generator.

## How does a bubble curtain work?

**A bubble curtain is a wall of air bubbles, pumped from a perforated hose or ring on the bed, that surrounds the piling and interrupts underwater sound.** The change from water to air at each bubble reflects, scatters and absorbs sound energy, so less of it travels out into the surrounding water. Performance varies with water depth, current and the frequency of the noise.

- *Commonly used for:* impact piling in rivers, docks and harbours; offshore wind foundations; dredging and other underwater works near sensitive fish and marine mammals
- *Pros:* treats underwater noise, which nothing on the surface can; can help discharge marine licence conditions; independent of the pile foundation itself
- *Cons:* performance depends heavily on depth, current and design; needs a compressor and often a separate vessel, which make noise of their own; does nothing for airborne noise

**How much difference does it make?** It depends a great deal on the site. A Cefas evidence review notes that in German offshore wind farms, noise abatement systems reduced piling noise emissions by 15 dB overall. A Belgian offshore trial, by contrast, measured only a single-digit reduction from a single big bubble curtain, with a double-figure reduction only when it was combined with a second system. In its updated advice on disturbance ranges for harbour porpoise, JNCC treats piling with noise abatement of at least 10 dB as a separate case from unmitigated piling.

Closer to home, a bubble curtain around 91 m long was deployed around impact piling during a Tyneside dock redevelopment to protect migrating salmon, described at the time as a UK first for this use. For underwater work, ask for the predicted reduction at your site and how it will be monitored, rather than relying on a headline figure.

## How do you screen airborne noise from birds and homes?

Above the waterline, waterside works behave much like any other site, with two complications: the receptors are often wildlife on open mudflats with nothing in the way, and the work is often on a quay, jetty or bridge deck where digging in foundations isn't an option.

**A real example.** A contractor building a bridge over mudflats on the Humber, which is protected as a Special Protection Area, Special Area of Conservation, Ramsar site and SSSI, had to stop work because of birds using the mudflats. Hushtec proposed a **6 m RapidWall along the bridge parapet** to screen the works from the mudflats. In the end the contractor chose to pause construction until the nesting season was over. That is often the lowest-risk route with the regulator, but it costs time.

The lessons for anyone planning similar work:

- **Height and position matter most.** Birds on open mudflats see straight across the site, so the wall has to block the line of sight from the work to the birds. As a first estimate, Hushtec's working rule is that an acoustic screen should be about 1.5 m higher than the top of the noise source. It is not a substitute for a noise or ecology assessment. See [how high an acoustic wall needs to be](/articles/how-high-acoustic-wall-generator-plant-noise).
- **Sight matters as well as sound.** Birds react to the movement of people and plant, not just noise. A solid wall helps with both.
- **Agree it before the season starts.** A wall only lifts a restriction if Natural England or the MMO accepts it as mitigation. Propose it in the licence application or method statement, not once birds are already nesting or feeding.
- **Check the structure.** A tall wall on a bridge deck or quay edge needs a site-specific check of wind loading, deck loading and how it is held in place.

## What are the limits?

- **Acoustic walls do nothing for underwater noise.** For piling and dredging in the water, the underwater controls have to be dealt with separately.
- **Bubble curtains do nothing for airborne noise**, and their own compressors and vessels add some noise.
- **Neither lifts a seasonal restriction by itself.** The regulator decides what counts as acceptable mitigation.
- **Marine conditions are harsh.** Salt, wind, UV and tides wear equipment, so check that anything specified is made for marine use.

## What to ask for

1. Which noise does the licence or consent actually control: underwater, airborne or both?
2. Which species or receptors is it protecting, and at what times of year?
3. For underwater work, what reduction is predicted from the bubble curtain or other system, and how will it be monitored?
4. For airborne noise, does the wall block the line of sight from the noisiest plant to the birds or homes?
5. Has the mitigation been agreed with Natural England or the MMO before the restricted season?
