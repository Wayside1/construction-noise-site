## Why is piling so hard to screen?

Driven piling, where a hammer strikes a precast concrete or steel pile into the ground, produces sharp, repeated impacts that carry a long way. The problem for screening is **height**: the hammer sits high on the rig's leader, well above a 2 m fence line, so a perimeter barrier often hides the rig's tracks but not the noise.

There are three main ways to tackle it, and they're often used together.

| Option | Typical reduction | When the rig moves | Best for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rig-mounted shroud round the hammer** | About 7 dB (7.2 dB at 5 m) | Travels with the rig | Sites with frequent rig moves |
| **Freestanding enclosure round the active pile** | 15–21 dB | A few hours to relocate | Piles nearest homes, sensitive times |
| **Tall perimeter wall** | Depends on height and position | Stays at the boundary | Protecting a whole frontage; see [barriers up to 8 m](/options/acoustic-barriers-up-to-8m) |

## How much does a rig-mounted shroud help?

**A rig-mounted shroud is an acoustic cover made to fit round the pile hammer, so it moves up and down the leader with it.** Field comparisons give a clear picture:

| Distance | No shroud | Half shroud | Full shroud |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 m | 100.0 dB | 93.2 dB | 92.8 dB |
| 10 m | 94.7 dB | 88.3 dB | 88.2 dB |
| 15 m | 89.9 dB | 86.7 dB | 82.5 dB |

*Indicative results from separate trials: full shroud at Woodhall Spa, half shroud at Ramsey, no-shroud figures from a published baseline. Results vary with rig, pile, ground and measurement conditions.*

About 7 dB is a clearly noticeable improvement, and because the shroud stays on the hammer it keeps working as the rig moves round the site.

## The common mistake: wrapping barrier panels round the rig

It's tempting to hang standard fence-style acoustic panels round a piling rig. It does very little. The hammer is still high and exposed, the panels leave gaps everywhere, and they aren't designed for the movement and loads around a working rig. Piling needs a **shroud made for the hammer**, an **enclosure round the active pile**, or a **wall tall enough to hide the hammer**.

## Limits

- **Results are site-specific.** Rig, hammer, pile type and ground all change the numbers.
- **No effect on vibration.** Ground vibration from driven piling has to be managed by method (for example rotary bored or CFA piling instead of driven), plant and hours.
- **Fit is specific.** A rig-mounted shroud must suit the rig and hammer configuration.
- **Enclosures cost time.** Moving a freestanding enclosure between piles slows production, so it's usually kept for the piles nearest homes.

## Pros and cons of a rig-mounted shroud

**Pros**

- Treats the noise at source, in every direction
- Moves with the rig, so no relocation time
- Protects the site team as well as neighbours
- Combines well with perimeter walls and enclosures

**Cons**

- Smaller reduction than a full enclosure round the pile
- Must fit the specific rig and hammer
- Doesn't help with vibration

## What to ask for

1. Which piling method is being used, and was a quieter method considered?
2. Is the hammer shrouded, and is the shroud made for this rig?
3. Will the piles nearest homes have an enclosure or extra screening?
4. What does the Section 61 consent or noise management plan commit to?
5. What hours will piling run, and how will neighbours be told in advance?
