## What is a rock breaker shroud?

**A rock breaker shroud is an acoustic cover that wraps round the body of a hydraulic breaker (also called a hydraulic hammer or pecker) fitted to an excavator.** It's made from heavy, sound-blocking material with an absorbent lining, and fits around the breaker housing while leaving the tool point free to work.

Hydraulic breakers are among the loudest tools on a site and one of the commonest causes of complaints. They're used for demolition, breaking out concrete and rock, utility trenches and foundations.

**Commonly used for:**

- Demolition and breaking out slabs near homes and offices
- Rock breaking in quarries, cuttings and foundations
- Utility and roadworks in residential streets
- Night or weekend work where noise limits are tight

## Why treat the noise at the machine?

A barrier protects only the area behind it. A breaker moves around the site and often works above barrier height, so a fence-line barrier soon stops helping. A shroud:

- **Reduces noise in every direction**, not just towards one boundary
- **Moves with the machine**, so there's nothing to relocate
- **Helps the operator and site team** as well as the neighbours

## How much difference does it make?

| Test | Without shroud | With shroud |
|---|---|---|
| Hushtec rock breaker test | 92.4 dB | 77.4 dB |

That's a 15 dB reduction. As a guide, 10 dB sounds roughly half as loud. Results elsewhere depend on the breaker, what it's breaking, the distance and the surroundings.

## Limits

- **The tip is still exposed.** The tool has to strike the material, and that impact makes noise.
- **No effect on vibration.** Ground vibration from breaking needs to be managed by method, plant size and hours.
- **Fit matters.** A loose or damaged shroud leaks sound. It must suit the breaker model, leave hoses free and allow for servicing and heat.
- **Very close neighbours** may still need screening round the work area, or a quieter method such as crushing or cutting.

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Cuts noise at source, in all directions
- Stays with the breaker as it moves
- Quick win on a site where one machine causes most complaints
- Can be combined with barriers and scheduling

**Cons**

- Doesn't silence the impact at the tip
- Needs to suit the specific breaker
- Must be checked and maintained to keep working
- Does nothing for vibration

## What to ask for

1. Is the shroud made for this breaker model, and how is it fixed?
2. What measured reduction has it achieved, and under what conditions?
3. How does it allow for hoses, heat and servicing?
4. Who checks it for wear and damage, and how often?
5. What else is in the noise plan: hours, screening, quieter methods?
