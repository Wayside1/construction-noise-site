## Why go taller than 2 metres?

A barrier only works if it blocks the line of sight between the noise and the listener. Standard temporary fencing stops at about 2 m, which is fine for ground-level neighbours and low plant. It does almost nothing when:

- **The noise source is tall:** piling hammers high on the leader, crushers, concrete pumps, cranes, large generators
- **Neighbours look down on the site:** flats, offices and houses with upstairs windows
- **The site is in a city centre:** surrounded by taller buildings, where sound reflects back and forth

In those cases you need either a taller wall or to treat the noise at the machine (see [pile rig shrouds](/options/pile-rig-shrouds) and [rock breaker shrouds](/options/rock-breaker-shrouds)). Often the best answer is both.

## How high does the wall need to be?

Hushtec's working rule is that **an acoustic screen should be about 1.5 m higher than the top of the noise source**. So a 2.5 m generator needs a wall of about 4 m. This is a first estimate, not a standard. If windows overlook the source, the wall needs to be taller again, and a noise assessment to BS 5228-1 should confirm the height. Our guide [How high does an acoustic wall need to be?](/articles/how-high-acoustic-wall-generator-plant-noise) works through examples.

## What are the options for a tall acoustic wall?

| Option | How it works | Commonly used for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Freestanding acoustic wall** | Modular frame and acoustic panels standing on the ground | Piling, demolition, generators, data centres, perimeters with buried services | Needs space for the base; check the wind rating for the height |
| **Scaffold-mounted barriers** | Acoustic panels or curtains fixed to a designed scaffold | Façade work, sites already scaffolded | Scaffold design must allow for wind on the acoustic face; ties and footings |
| **Stacked container wall** | Shipping or site containers stacked as a screen | Long-term perimeters, sites that need the storage | Hard, reflective faces unless lined; gaps between units; crane needed to move |

All three can work. The right one depends on height, how long it's needed, how often it moves, the ground, and whether the faces need to absorb sound or just block it.

## What good looks like

- **Height** set from a noise assessment, using the 1.5 m rule only as a first check
- **Wind design** for the actual height and location, with a stated wind rating
- **No gaps** between modules or at the base
- **Absorbent face towards the works**, so noise doesn't bounce off the wall and back over the site
- **Close to the noise**, with returns or a U-shape round the noisiest area where possible
- **Tested panel figures:** an Rw for sound insulation and an αw for absorption

## Limits

- **It's airborne noise only.** A wall does nothing for ground vibration.
- **Very tall sources may still be visible.** A piling hammer near the top of a leader can sit above even an 8 m wall; a [shroud on the hammer](/options/pile-rig-shrouds) or an enclosure round the active pile may be needed as well.
- **Space and access.** Freestanding bases and container walls take up ground. Plan for access, deliveries and emergency routes.

## What to ask for

1. What height has the noise assessment recommended, and to which windows?
2. What is the system's wind rating at that height, and who designed it?
3. Does it need anchors, footings or excavation, and are there buried services?
4. What are the panel's Rw and αw, and are the faces absorbent?
5. How quickly can it be extended, moved or reconfigured as the work changes?
