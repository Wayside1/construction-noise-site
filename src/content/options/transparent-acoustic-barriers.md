## What is a transparent acoustic barrier?

**A transparent acoustic barrier is a clear, dense material that blocks sound while letting people see through it and letting light pass.** It does the same basic job as any acoustic barrier, stopping sound passing through, but without creating a solid wall.

There are two main types:

- **Clear acoustic curtains:** flexible, heavy, see-through sheet (often mass-loaded vinyl), supplied on a roll or cut to size with eyelets. They hang from fencing, scaffold or a frame, so they can move with the work.
- **Rigid clear panels:** acrylic or polycarbonate sheets in a frame. Clearer and tougher, but heavier, and usually used for longer-term or permanent installations.

## When does see-through make sense?

**Commonly used for:**

- **Safety sightlines:** next to footpaths, junctions and site entrances, so drivers, plant operators and pedestrians can see each other
- **Shops and businesses** next to works, which need to stay visible to customers
- **Neighbours' daylight:** where a solid barrier would shade windows or gardens
- **Supervision:** work areas a supervisor or banksman needs to watch
- **Inside buildings:** see-through dividers that split noisy machinery from quieter areas in factories and workshops
- **Public-facing sites:** where a solid wall would feel hostile or attract graffiti

## What are the limits?

The main trade-off is **absorption**. Clear materials have hard, smooth faces, so they reflect sound rather than soak it up. A solid barrier with an absorbent face stops noise bouncing around the site; a clear one doesn't. On a tight site, or where there are buildings on both sides, that reflected sound can matter.

The usual answer is to **mix the two**: clear panels or windows where people need to see, and absorbent panels everywhere else. Some systems are designed to combine clear and absorbent sections in one wall.

The other limits are the same as for any barrier:

- It has to **block the direct path** between the noise and the listener; being see-through doesn't change that
- **No gaps** at joints or the base
- **Height:** fence-mounted curtains are limited to fence height, about 2 m
- **Scratches and dirt** reduce clarity over time; rigid panels need cleaning and can be damaged

## Pros and cons

**Pros**

- Keeps sightlines for safety and supervision
- Lets daylight through to neighbours and work areas
- Keeps shops and businesses visible
- Feels less closed-in for the public

**Cons**

- Reflects sound instead of absorbing it
- Clear curtains are heavy for their size, and rigid panels more so
- Clarity can drop with scratches, dirt and weather
- Fewer published test figures than standard barriers, so check the data

## What to ask for

1. What is the material's tested Rw, from which lab and to which standard?
2. Is it a flexible curtain or a rigid panel, and how is it fixed?
3. Where are absorbent sections needed to stop reflections?
4. How are joints and the base sealed?
5. How does the material hold up to UV, weather and cleaning?
