**BS 5228:2009+A1:2014** is the British Standard Code of Practice for *Noise and Vibration Control on Construction and Open Sites*. It is the primary technical guidance that councils, contractors, and courts refer to when assessing construction noise.

## What is BS 5228?

BS 5228 is split into two parts:
- **Part 1: Noise** – covers noise prediction, assessment, and control measures
- **Part 2: Vibration** – covers vibration from construction activities

It is not a legal requirement in itself, but it is approved as a code of practice under the **Control of Pollution Act 1974** and is referenced in local planning conditions, making it effectively the benchmark on most significant sites.

## Key Principles

### 1. Noise Prediction
Before work begins, contractors should predict noise levels at nearby sensitive receptors (homes, schools, hospitals) using guidance in BS 5228. This informs the Construction Management Plan (CMP).

### 2. Best Practicable Means (BPM)
Contractors are expected to use **Best Practicable Means** to minimise noise. BS 5228 provides the framework for what BPM looks like in practice:
- Selecting quieter plant and equipment
- Acoustic screening close to the noisiest plant and at site boundaries
- Scheduling the noisiest work during daytime hours only
- Regular maintenance of equipment

### 3. Assessing Significance (the ABC method)
BS 5228-1 Annex E gives an example method for judging whether construction noise is significant at a home. It compares predicted construction noise with the existing ambient level. Daytime thresholds (weekdays 07:00–19:00, Saturdays 07:00–13:00, LAeq at the facade) are:

| Category | Existing ambient level | Daytime threshold |
|------|----------------------|------|
| A | Quietest areas | 65 dB |
| B | Ambient about 5 dB above Category A | 70 dB |
| C | Ambient equal to or above Category B | 75 dB |

Lower thresholds apply in evenings, at weekends and at night. Your council may set its own limits through a Section 60 notice or Section 61 consent.

### 4. Notification to Neighbours
Best practice under BS 5228 includes notifying nearby residents in advance of particularly noisy work, giving them the site manager's contact details.

## Section 61 Prior Consent

Contractors carrying out significant works are strongly advised to apply for **Section 61 Prior Consent** from the local council before starting. This formalises agreement on working hours, methods, and noise limits — providing legal protection against a Section 60 notice if complaints arise.
