A well-written complaint letter to your council can significantly increase your chances of a prompt and effective response. Here's how to write one.

## Before You Write

Make sure you have gathered:
- A noise incident log (dates, times, duration, type of noise)
- Decibel measurements where possible
- Any photos or videos of the site
- Details of any previous contact with the site manager or council

## Structure of an Effective Letter

### 1. Your Details
Include your full name, address, and contact details at the top.

### 2. Clear Subject Line
E.g. *"Formal Noise Complaint – Construction at [Site Address]"*

### 3. Summary of the Problem
State clearly when the noise started, how frequent it is, and how it is affecting you (sleep disruption, inability to work from home, health impacts, etc.).

### 4. Evidence
Reference your noise log and any measurements. Attach copies as appendices.

### 5. Legal Reference
Mention the **Environmental Protection Act 1990** and request an investigation under Section 80 (Statutory Nuisance). This signals you know your rights and are serious.

### 6. What You Are Asking For
Be specific. Request:
- An Environmental Health Officer investigation
- A case reference number
- A response within a defined timeframe (e.g. 10 working days)

### 7. Escalation Notice
State that if the council does not act, you will consider applying to a magistrates' court under **Section 82 of the EPA 1990**.

## After Sending

- Send via email so you have a timestamped record
- Follow up if you have not heard within 10 working days
- Keep copies of all correspondence
