Construction sites generate some of the loudest and most persistent noise in our environment — and the impact on local wildlife can be profound. While human noise complaints focus on health and wellbeing, UK law also recognises that wildlife deserves protection from the disruption caused by construction activity.

## Why Noise Matters to Wildlife

Animals rely on sound for survival — to communicate, find mates, detect predators, and navigate. Persistent construction noise at levels above 60–70 dB can:

- Disrupt communication between animals (birdsong, bat echolocation)
- Cause chronic stress responses, affecting feeding and reproduction
- Force animals to abandon territories or nesting sites
- Disorient nocturnal species that rely on quiet to hunt

The effects are not always visible — but they can be long-lasting and ecologically damaging.

## Badgers – A Specially Protected Species

**Badgers are one of the most significant wildlife concerns on UK construction sites.** Under the **Protection of Badgers Act 1992**, it is a criminal offence to:

- Wilfully kill, injure or take a badger
- Interfere with a badger sett, including damaging or obstructing it or disturbing a badger while it is occupying a sett
- Cause a dog to enter a badger sett

Disturbance from heavy works close to an occupied sett can count as interference — even if no physical damage is done — particularly during the main breeding season (December to June).

### What Developers Must Do

Before any construction near a suspected badger sett, developers are expected to:

1. Commission a **badger survey** by a suitably experienced ecologist
2. Apply for a **badger licence** from Natural England (or the relevant body in Wales or Scotland) if interference with a sett is unavoidable
3. Implement a **Badger Method Statement** setting out how disturbance will be minimised
4. Avoid works near active setts during the main breeding season where possible

If you suspect a badger sett near a construction site is being disturbed without a licence, you can report it to the police (it is a wildlife crime), **Natural England** or the **Badger Trust**.

## Nesting Birds – Seasonal Protections

All wild birds, their nests, and eggs are protected under the **Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981**. It is illegal to intentionally take, damage or destroy an active nest.

Construction noise and vibration during **nesting season (typically March to August)** can:

- Cause parent birds to abandon nests
- Stress developing chicks
- Disrupt the feeding patterns of ground-nesting species such as lapwings and skylarks

### What This Means in Practice

Responsible developers will commission a **nesting bird check** before clearance works in the nesting season. If active nests are found within or adjacent to the site, works are usually halted or restricted in that area until the young have fledged.

Permitted working hours under the **Control of Pollution Act 1974** do not override wildlife protections — a developer can be compliant with noise law and still be committing a wildlife crime.

## Bats – The Most Legally Protected Mammals

All UK bat species are protected under the **Conservation of Habitats and Species Regulations 2017**. It is illegal to:

- Deliberately disturb bats
- Damage or destroy a bat roost (even if bats are not present)

Construction noise and light pollution near bat roosts — particularly at night — can prevent bats from roosting or foraging. Any development near a suspected roost requires a **bat survey** and, if disturbance is unavoidable, a **European Protected Species (EPS) licence**.

## Other Species of Concern

- **Hedgehogs** – increasingly rare, hedgehog populations are threatened by habitat loss and construction disturbance. While not legally protected in the same way, local planning conditions sometimes require mitigation.
- **Great Crested Newts** – one of the most strictly protected species in the UK. Surveys and licences are required if newts may be affected.
- **Water Voles** – protected under the Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981. Often found near drainage ditches on construction sites.

## What You Can Do

If you believe construction near you is disturbing protected wildlife:

- Report concerns to **Natural England** (0300 060 3900) or via their website
- Contact the **RSPCA** (0300 1234 999) for injured or distressed animals
- Raise the issue with the **Local Planning Authority** — wildlife disturbance may breach planning conditions
- Contact the **Badger Trust** (badgertrust.org.uk) for badger-specific concerns

Construction companies have a legal duty to protect wildlife, not just to minimise noise for human neighbours. If proper surveys and licences have not been obtained, that is a serious regulatory failure worth reporting.
